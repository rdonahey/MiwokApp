Appendix: Bonus Deflection Example

This bonus example is taken from Example 5.8.3.1 (based on details from Example 5.2.2.3) of the PCI Design Handbook,\({}^{12}\) where immediate deflection from live load is calculated for a Class T double tee (8DT24) that is prestressed with the strands harped at midspan (single-point depressed strands). The beam is simply supported with a span \(\ell =70\) ft.

Two alternative procedures are presented for direct calculation of immediate deflection based on either a parabolic or trapezoidal approximation for the distribution of net curvature along the member span. Comparison is made with deflection computed using integration of curvature based on the effective eccentricity \(e_e\) and effective moment of inertia \(I_e\) corresponding to each section along the member span (with \(e_g\) and \(I_g\) used in the uncracked regions). Plus, results are presented for deflection calculation using the PCI bilinear load-deflection response. Calculation in some parts is carried out with more precision than needed (in terms of significant figures) to facilitate comparison of results.

Beam gross section properties

The cross section of the 24 in. deep PCI double tee shown in Fig. A1 has the following section properties for the gross (uncracked) section.

A\({}_{g}\) = 401 in.\({}^{2}\) and I\({}_{g}\) = 20,985 in.\({}^{4}\)

y\({}_{top}\) = 6.85 in. and y\({}_{bot}\) = 17.15 in.

w\({}_{sw}\) = 150 lb/ft\({}^{3}\) x 401 in.\({}^{2}\)\(\mathrm{\div}\)12\(\mathrm{\div}\)12 = 418 lb/ft (distributed line load for self-weight)

Figure A1: Cross section of PCI double tee (8DT24)

Prestressing details

The beam is prestressed with twelve 1/2 in. diameter, 270 ksi, low-relaxation strands. This gives A\({}_{ps}\) = 12 x 0.153 = 1.836 in.\({}^{2}\)

The strands have a single harping point at midspan, giving a straight tendon with variable eccentricity. Eccentricities are as follows:

e\({}_{g,e}\)\({}_{nd}\) = 5.48 in. (eccentricity of prestressing force at end of span)

e\({}_{g,c}\) = 13.90 in. (eccentricity of prestressing force at midspan)

q = (13.90-5.48)/(70 x 12/2) = 20.05 x 10\({}^{-3}\) rads (angle of inclination for centroid of prestressing strands)

e\({}_{g}\)(x) = e\({}_{g,e}\)\({}_{nd}\) + q\({}_{\ }\)x (eccentricity at distance x from end of span for x \(\mathrm{\le}\) \(\ell\) /2)

e\({}_{g}\)(\(0.4\ell\)) = 5.48 + 20.05 x 10\({}^{-3}\) x (0.4 x 70 x 12) = 12.216 in. (eccentricity at \(0.4\ell\))

The effective depth d\({}_{p}\) of the prestressing steel and moment of inertia of the cracked section I\({}_{cr}\) vary along the member span as the eccentricity changes.

d\({}_{p}\) = 6.85 + 13.90 = 20.75 in. at \(0.5\ell\)

d\({}_{p}\) = 6.85 + 12.216 = 19.066 in. at \(0.4\ell\)

Initial prestress level = 0.75f\({}_{pu}\) and estimated loss equals 20%

f\({}_{se}\) = (1-0.20)(0.75)(270) = 162.0 ksi

P\({}_{e}\) = A\({}_{ps}\) x f\({}_{se}\) = 1.836 x 162.0 = 297.4 kip

Concrete design properties

The concrete has a specified compressive strength \(f'_c=5000\) psi, giving \(f_r=7.5\sqrt{f_c'}=7.5\sqrt{5000}=530\) psi and \(E_c=33{w_c}^{1.5}\sqrt{f_c'}=33{\left(145\right)}^{1.5}\sqrt{5000}/1000=4074\) ksi. Using w\({}_{c}\) = 145 pcf for normalweight concrete gives \(E_c\approx 57,000\sqrt{f_c'}\). The modular ratio \(n_p=E_p/E_c=28,500/4074=7.0\) (assuming E\({}_{p}\) = 28,500 ksi).

Member loads

member self-weight w\({}_{sw}\) = 418 lb/ft (from beam gross section properties)

superimposed dead load w\({}_{sd}\) = 10 lb/ft\({}^{2}\) x 8 ft = 80 lb/ft

total dead load w\({}_{D}\) = 418 + 80 = 498 lb/ft

live load w\({}_{L}\) = 35 lb/ft\({}^{2}\) x 8 ft = 280 lb/ft

Service load moments

The critical moment occurs at midspan (\(\ell /2\)) for beams with straight strands of constant eccentricity and is assumed to occur at \(0.4\ell\) for beams when the strands are depressed at midspan\({}^{\ }\)as with this example.\({}^{12}\) For the prestressed beam in this example, the actual critical section occurs at \(0.39\ell\) for the maximum value of total bottom stress f\({}_{b}\) in the concrete at the precompressed tension face, and at \(0.384\ell\) for the maximum value of M/M\({}_{cr}\) (details are provided further on in Aside 1). Figure A2 plots the distribution of f\({}_{b}\)\({}_{\ }\)/f\({}_{r}\) and M/M\({}_{cr}\) along the member span. Negative values of the bottom stress for f\({}_{b}\) (indicating compression) that occur in the region near the end support are not plotted in this figure.

Service load moments at midspan (\(\ell /2\)) are as follows:

\(M_D\left(\ell /2\right)=w_D{\ell }^2/8=498\div 12\div 1000\times {\left(70\times 12\right)}^2/8=3660.3\) kip-in.

\(M_L\left(\ell /2\right)=w_L{\ell }^2/8=280\div 12\div 1000\times {\left(70\times 12\right)}^2/8=2058.0\) kip-in.

\(M_a=M_{D+L}\left(\ell /2\right)=M_D\left(\ell /2\right)+M_L\left(\ell /2\right)=5718.3\) kip-in.

Service load moments at the critical section (\(0.4\ell\)) are equal to 0.96 times the moment at midspan based on a parabolic distribution of moment from the uniformly distributed loads.

\(M_D(0.4\ell )=0.96M_D(0.5\ell )=0.96\times 3660.3=3513.9\) kip-in.

\(M_L(0.4\ell )=0.96M_L(0.5\ell )=0.96\times 2058.0=1975.7\) kip-in.

\(M\left(0.4\ell \right)=M_{D+L}\left(0.4\ell \right)=0.96M_a=0.96\times 5718.3=5489.\mathrm{6}\) kip-in.

where \(M_a\) is the total dead plus live load moment at midspan (\(0.5\ell\)).

Figure A2: Distribution of f\({}_{b}\)\({}_{\ }\)/f\({}_{r}\) and M/M\({}_{cr}\) along the member span.

Critical section properties (at 0.4l)

When deflection is computed directly, a constant value of member stiffness EI and eccentricity e of the prestressing force is assumed based on the effective moment of inertia I\({}_{e}\) and effective eccentricity e\({}_{e}\) at the critical section located at \(0.4\ell\). The section is cracked at \(0.4\ell\), but barely cracked at midspan (\(0.5\ell\)) as observed from Fig. A2.

Curvatures at the critical section

Corresponding curvatures at the critical section (\(0.4\ell\)) are computed using guidance from Fig. A3 that summarizes the relevant equations for curvature needed to calculate deflection. Values for the section properties and curvatures are summarized in Table A1 for convenience. \[\phi \left(0.4\ell \mathrm{\ }\right)=\frac{M\left(0.4\ell \mathrm{\ }\right)-P_ee_e(0.4\ell \mathrm{\ })}{E_cI_e(0.4\ell \mathrm{\ })}=\frac{5489.6-297.4\times 11.30}{4074\times 14,436}=36.20\times {10}^{-6}\ 1/\mathrm{in.}\] \[{\phi }_{p,g}\left(0.4\ell \mathrm{\ }\right)=\frac{P_ee_g(0.4\ell )}{E_cI_g}=\frac{297.4\times 12.216}{4074\times 20,985}=42.495\times {10}^{-6}\ 1/\mathrm{in.}\] \[{\phi }_{net}\left(0.4\ell \mathrm{\ }\right)=\phi \left(0.4\ell \right)+{\phi }_{p,g}\left(0.4\ell \right)=\left(36.20+42.495\right)\times {10}^{-6}=78.695\times {10}^{-6}\ 1/\mathrm{in.}\] \[{\phi }_D\left(0.4\ell \mathrm{\ }\right)=\frac{M_D\left(0.4\ell \mathrm{\ }\right)}{E_cI_g}=\frac{3513.9}{4074\times 20,985}=41.10\times {10}^{-6}\ 1/\mathrm{in.}\] \[{\phi }_L\left(0.4\ell \right)={\phi }_{net}\left(0.4\ell \mathrm{\ }\right)-{\phi }_D\left(0.4\ell \mathrm{\ }\right)=\left(78.695-41.10\right)\times {10}^{-6}=37.595\times {10}^{-6}\ 1/\mathrm{in.}\]

\[I_e\left(x\right)=\frac{I_{cr}\left(x\right)}{1-{\left(\frac{{\lambda }_{cr}M_{cr}(x)}{M(x)}\right)}^2\left(1-\frac{I_{cr}\left(x\right)}{I_g}\right)}\] \[e_e\left(x\right)=\left(\frac{{\lambda }_{cr}M_{cr}(x)}{M(x)}\right)\left(\frac{I_e\left(x\right)}{I_g}\right)e_g\left(x\right)+\left[1-\left(\frac{{\lambda }_{cr}M_{cr}(x)}{M(x)}\right)\right]\left(\frac{I_e\left(x\right)}{I_{cr}(x)}\right)e_{cr}\left(x\right)\] \[{\phi }_{pe}\left(x\right)=\frac{P_ee_e(x)}{E_cI_e(x)}\ \ \ \mathrm{and\ \ }\ {\phi }_{p,g}\left(x\right)=\frac{P_ee_g(x)}{E_cI_g}\] \[\phi \left(x\right)=\frac{M(x)}{E_cI_e(x)}-{\phi }_{pe}\left(x\right)=\frac{M\left(x\right)-P_ee_e(x)}{E_cI_e(x)}\] \[{\phi }_{p+D}\left(x\right)=\frac{M_D\left(x\right)}{E_cI_g}-{\phi }_{p,g}(x)\] \[{\phi }_D\left(x\right)=\frac{M_D\left(x\right)}{E_cI_g}\] \[{\phi }_{net}\left(x\right)=\phi \left(x\right)+{\phi }_{p,g}\left(x\right)={\phi }_D\left(x\right)+{\phi }_L\left(x\right)\] \[{\phi }_L\left(x\right)={\phi }_{net}\left(x\right)-{\phi }_D\left(x\right)=\phi \left(x\right)+{\phi }_{p,g}\left(x\right)-\frac{M_D\left(x\right)}{E_cI_g}\]

Parabolic approximation for net curvature

Assuming a parabolic curve for the net curvature \({\phi }_{net}\) running through the value at \(0.4\ell\) and having a maximum value at midspan (\(0.5\ell\)) as shown in Fig. A5 gives \[{\phi }_{net}\left(0.5\ell \mathrm{\ }\right)\approx {\phi }_{net}\left(0.4\ell \mathrm{\ }\right)/0.96=(78.695/0.96)\times {10}^{-6}\ 1/\mathrm{in.=81.97}\times {10}^{-6}\ 1/\mathrm{in.}\] In a similar fashion, a parabolic curve fitted to the curvature value from live load at \(0.4\ell\) gives \[{\phi }_L\left(0.5\ell \mathrm{\ }\right)\approx {\phi }_L\left(0.4\ell \mathrm{\ }\right)/0.96=37.595\times {10}^{-6}/0.96=39.16\times {10}^{-6}\ 1/\mathrm{in.}\]

Deflections based on net curvature using parabolic approximation

Knowing that \(K_M=5/48\) when the curvature is parabolic, gives \[{\Delta }_{i,L}=K_M{\phi }_L\left(0.5\ell \mathrm{\ }\right){\ell }^2=\frac{5}{48}\times 39.16\times {10}^{-6}\times {(70\times 12)}^2=2.88\ \mathrm{in.}\] and is about 20% greater than the value of \({\Delta }_{i,L}=2.39\ \mathrm{in.}\) when curvature is integrated. Integration takes account of the uncracked regions of the beam (using \(e_g(x)\) and \(I_g\)) as well as for changes in \(e_e(x)\) and \(I_e(x)\) in the cracked regions. Figures A2 and A4 indicate the beam is cracked near the midspan region for \(0.277\lt x/\ell \lt 0.723\)). Alternatively, \[{\Delta }_{net}=\frac{5}{48}{\phi }_{net}\left(0.5\ell \mathrm{\ }\right){\ell }^2=\frac{5}{48}\times 81.97\times {10}^{-6}\times {(70\times 12)}^2=6.025\ \mathrm{in.}\]

Alternative trapezoidal approximation for net curvature and deflection calculations

Estimating deflection by approximating the net curvature with a trapezoidal curve as shown in Fig. A6 (corresponding to the shape for an elastic beam under two-point loading assuming the loading points are located at \(0.4\ell\) from each support), gives \[{\Delta }_{net}=K_M{\phi }_{net}\left(0.4\ell \mathrm{\ }\right){\ell }^2=0.0983\times 78.695\times {10}^{-6}\times {(70\times 12)}^2=5.46\ \mathrm{in.}\] \[{\mathrm{where}\ K}_M=\left[3-4{\left(\frac{a}{\ell }\right)}^2\right]/24=\left[3-4{\left(0.4\right)}^2\right]/24=4.72/48=0.0983\] \[{\Delta }_{i,L}={\Delta }_{net}-{\Delta }_{i,D}=5.46-3.15=2.31\ \mathrm{in.}\] \[{\Delta }_a={\Delta }_{net}-{\Delta }_{p,g}=5.46-3.40=2.06\ \mathrm{in.}\] These estimated values of 2.31 in. for \({\Delta }_{i,L}\) and 2.06 in. for \({\Delta }_a\) are closer to the integrated values of deflection (2.39 in. for \({\Delta }_{i,L}\) and 2.14 in. for \({\Delta }_a\)) but now underestimate deflection by 3 to 4%. Compare this with the estimate using a parabolic curvature, where deflections are overestimated by about 20 to 23%.

Further investigation with this type of tendon profile is needed for different ratios of service load to cracking load before definite recommendations can be made regarding the merits of each approximation.

Aside 1: Location of Critical Section for Deflection Calculation

Determine the location for the maximum value of total stress f\({}_{b}\) at the bottom (precompressed) tensile face of the prestressed beam (refer to Fig. A2).

The angle of inclination q of the prestressing force given by \[\theta =2\left(e_{g,c}-e_{g,end}\right)/\ell\] is used to obtain the eccentricity e\({}_{g}\)(x) along the beam span

\(e_g\left(x\right)=e_{g,end}+\) \(\theta x\) for \(x\le \ell /2\)

For the bottom stress f\({}_{b}\) in the section for the prestress force plus dead and live load (where compression is negative and tension is positive) \[f_b\left(x\right)=-\frac{P_e}{A_g}-\frac{P_ee_g\left(x\right)y_{bot}}{I_g}+\frac{M(x)y_{bot}}{I_g}\] where \(M\left(x\right)=4M_a\left[\left(x/\ell \right)-{(x/\ell )}^2\right]\) and \(M_a=w{\ell }^2/8\) at midspan

Setting \(\frac{d}{dx}f_b\left(x\right)=0\) identifies the location of maximum stress for \(f_b\), giving \[x/\ell =0.5-P_e\theta \ \ell /\left(8M_a\right)=0.5-P_e\left(e_{g,c}-e_{g,end}\right)/\left(4M_a\right)\] \(x/\ell =0.39\) for \(P_e=297.4\) kip, \(\theta =0.02005\) rads, \(\ell =70\) ft, and \(M_a=5718.3\) kip-in.

Similarly, the location for the maximum value of \(M\left(x\right)/M_{cr}\left(x\right)\) occurs at

\(x/\ell =\sqrt{{\omega }^2+\omega }-\omega\), with \[\omega =\frac{e_{g,end}+I_g/\left(A_gy_{bot}\right)+f_rI_g/\left(P_ey_{bot}\right)}{2\left(e_{g,c}-e_{g,end}\right)}=0.6361\] to give \(x/\ell =0.384\) which is only a few percent different than the location for the maximum stress value f\({}_{b}\).

Aside 2: PCI Bilinear Load-Deflection Response Revisited

Example 5.8.3.1 from the PCI Design Handbook\({}^{12}\) computes deflection using a bilinear moment-deflection relationship as shown in Fig. A7. The PCI solution starts off by comparing the rupture modulus f\({}_{r}\) with the computed stress at the bottom face f\({}_{b}\) for the moment at \(0.4\ell\) (where tensile stresses for f\({}_{b}\) are assumed to be greatest as discussed earlier). However, deflection is calculated at midspan (equivalent to using an approximation for the cracking moment at \(0.5\ell\) as shown in Fig. A7).

Figure 7: Calculation of immediate deflection from live load using the PCI bilinear load-deflection response

Stresses are computed at the bottom face for \(x=0.4\ell\). For the prestress load only:

\(f_{pe}\left(0.4\ell \right)=3710.75\ \mathrm{psi\ (C)}\) from before

For prestress plus dead and live loads (total stress): \[f_{b,tot}\left(0.4\ell \right)={-f}_{pe}\left(0.4\ell \right)+\frac{M_D\left(0.4\ell \right)y_{bot}}{I_g}+\frac{M_L\left(0.4\ell \right)y_{bot}}{I_g}=-3710.75+\left(\frac{3513.9\left(17.15\right)}{20,985}+\frac{1975.7\left(17.15\right)}{20,985}\right)\times 1000=-3710.75+2871.74+1614.64=775.63\ \mathrm{psi\ (T)}\gt f_r=530\ \mathrm{psi}\] This beam is a Class T component since the total tensile stress \(f_{b,tot}=775.63\) psi (T) at the bottom face (for the prestress plus dead and live loads) is greater than \(f_r\) and less than \(12\sqrt{f_c'}=848.5\) psi. The stress \(f_{b,p+D}\) from prestress plus dead load, which is equal to \(-3710.75+2871.74=839.01\ \mathrm{psi\ (C)}\), indicates the beam is uncracked for dead load only. The tensile stress \(f_{b,L}\) from the live load alone equals 1614.64 psi (T).

That part of the live load \(w_{L1}\) needed to cause cracking at \(0.4\ell\) (for a bottom tensile stress of 530 psi) corresponds to a computed bottom stress from live load \(f_{b,L1}=\mathrm{839.01+530.0=1369.01}\) psi (T). This stress results from a portion of the total live load (280 lb/ft) as follows. \[w_{L1}=\frac{f_{b,L1}}{f_{b,L}}w_L=\frac{1369.01}{1614.64}\times 280=237.40\ \mathrm{lb/ft}\] with a corresponding part of the live load moment \(M_{L1}\) at midspan. \[M_{L1}(0.5\ell )=\frac{w_{L1}{\ell }^2}{8}=\frac{237.40{\left(70\right)}^2}{8}\times \frac{12}{1000}=1744.9\ \mathrm{k-in.}\] The stress \(f_{b,L2}=f_{b,L}-f_{b,L1}\) from that part of the live load \(w_{L2}\) after cracking equals \(1614.64-1369.01=245.63\) psi (or \(f_{b,tot}-f_r=775.63-530=245.63\) psi). Similarly \[w_{L2}=\frac{f_{b,L2}}{f_{b,L}}w_L=\frac{245.63}{1614.64}\times 280=42.60\ \mathrm{lb/ft}\] \[M_{L2}(0.5\ell )=\frac{w_{L2}{\ell }^2}{8}=\frac{42.60{\left(70\right)}^2}{8}\times \frac{12}{1000}=313.1\ \mathrm{k-in.}\] Deflection is computed using the midspan (\(0.5\ell\)) value of \(d_p=20.75\) in. for \({\rho }_p=\) \(1.836/\left(96\times 20.75\right)=0.00092\), to give \(I_{cr}=n_pA_{ps}{d_p}^2\left(1-1.6\sqrt{n_p{\rho }_p}\right)=4822\) in.\({}^{4}\)

The deflection from live load up to cracking \({\Delta }_g\) (corresponding to deflection from the gross (uncracked) response) \[{\Delta }_g=\frac{5}{48}\frac{M_{L1}{\ell }^2}{E_cI_g}=\frac{5}{48}\frac{\left(1744.9\right){\left(70\times 12\right)}^2}{\left(4074\right)\left(20,985\right)}=1.50\ \mathrm{in.}\] and the deflection from live load after cracking \({\Delta }_{cr}\) (corresponding to deflection of the cracked response) \[{\Delta }_{cr}=\frac{5}{48}\frac{M_{L2}{\ell }^2}{E_cI_{cr}}=\frac{5}{48}\frac{\left(313.1\right){\left(70\times 12\right)}^2}{\left(4074\right)\left(4822\right)}=1.17\ \mathrm{in.}\] Total live load deflection \({\Delta }_{i,L}={\Delta }_g+{\Delta }_{cr}=1.50+1.17=2.67\) in.

The procedure followed above for computing deflection from live load is equivalent to using a fictitious cracking moment at midspan based on the cracking moment computed at \(0.4\ell\) divided by 0.96 (assuming a parabolic curve for distribution of the moment along the span) as shown in Fig. A7.

\(M_{cr}\left(0.4\ell \right)=5189.0\) k-in. from before, and \[M_{cr}\left(0.5\ell \right)\approx \frac{M_{cr}\left(0.4\ell \right)}{0.96}=\frac{5189.0}{0.96}=5405.2\ \mathrm{k-in.}\] to give

\(M_{L1}=M_{cr}\left(0.5\ell \right)-M_D\left(0.5\ell \right)=5405.2-3660.3=1744.9\) k-in. as before, and

\(M_{L2}=M_{D+L}\left(0.5\ell \right)-M_{cr}\left(0.5\ell \right)=5718.3-5405.2=313.1\) k-in. as before.

Computed values of deflection are slightly different from those in the PCI example\({}^{12}\) because of differences in the value used for \(E_c\) and roundoff error.
